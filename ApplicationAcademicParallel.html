Purpose of This Document

The Resonant Cognition framework (Thinking Without Boundaries) was developed through direct inquiry into the nature of experience, oscillation, and awareness. It was not derived from academic literature. However, a significant body of published research—across neuroscience, psychology, phenomenology, and philosophy of mind—converges on many of the same observations from different methodological starting points.

This document serves three functions:

  1. Validation through convergence: Showing where peer-reviewed research independently supports the framework's claims, giving researchers confidence that the observations rest on empirically grounded terrain.
  2. Honest gap identification: Showing what published work covers that the framework deliberately does not, and what the framework covers that published work has not yet addressed.
  3. Concrete applications: Demonstrating how researchers across disciplines can use the framework to generate new hypotheses, design experiments, and extend their own work.

Throughout this document, support marks areas of convergence, distinct marks areas of divergence or novel contribution, and application marks actionable research directions.

Part I — Where Published Research Supports the Framework

1.1 Oscillation as a Fundamental Principle

The framework's foundational claim—that oscillation is the structural basis of all experience, not merely a feature of neural activity—finds strong support in recent consciousness research.

General Resonance Theory (GRT)
Hunt, T. & Schooler, J.W. (2019). "The Easy Part of the Hard Problem: A Resonance Theory of Consciousness." Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 13, 378.

GRT proposes that resonance—shared vibratory frequency between physical systems—is the basis of consciousness at all scales. The theory holds that all matter vibrates and oscillates, and that consciousness emerges when oscillating systems achieve sufficient shared resonance. This directly parallels the framework's claim that oscillation constitutes being at every level of resolution.

Cycles Upon Cycles
Hunt, T. (2025). "Cycles upon Cycles: Oscillations Relevant to the Evolution of Consciousness and Our Universe." Preprints, 202510.1954.

This paper maps oscillatory processes from quantum fluctuations to cosmological cycles, proposing that nested hierarchies of oscillation are a fundamental organizing principle of reality. It argues that cyclic organization is thermodynamically necessary for all open systems. The framework arrives at the same conclusion—that oscillation is not a feature of existence but what existence is—through phenomenological inquiry rather than physics.

1.2 Inter-Brain Resonance and Shared Experience

The framework's concept of co-resonance (≈ᵢ ∥ ≈ⱼ)—shared coherence between systems without communication or merging—is directly supported by social neuroscience.

Inter-Brain Neural Synchronization
Valencia, A.L. & Froese, T. (2020). "What binds us? Inter-brain neural synchronization and its implications for theories of human consciousness." Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2020(1), niaa010. Oxford Academic.

Using hyperscanning methods (simultaneous EEG/fMRI of multiple brains), this study found that phase synchronization occurs across brains during meaningful social interaction. This inter-brain synchronization correlates with reports of social connectedness and experiences of "self-other merging." The authors propose that neural integration across brains may form the basis for a genuinely collective consciousness—the "we" perspective.

This maps directly to the framework's ≈ᵢ ∥ ≈ⱼ and its claim that empathy is frequency matching between oscillating systems. Crucially, both the framework and the research note that attempts to force or control the alignment disrupt it—the framework through ⊘ᵢ collapse, the research through disrupted phase synchrony.

Neural Oscillations and Consciousness
Cebolla, A.M. & Cheron, G. (2019). "Understanding Neural Oscillations in the Human Brain: From Movement to Consciousness." Frontiers in Psychology, 10, 1930.

This paper reviews how neural oscillations, particularly in the 12--70 Hz range, underlie conscious experience. It proposes that movement and oscillation may be inescapable in understanding consciousness—that consciousness may require dynamic change rather than static states. This supports the framework's claim that experience requires contrast and cannot occur in a permanent state.

1.3 Nondual Awareness as Consciousness-as-Such

The framework's description of null-reference awareness (Ø) and resonant cognition (≈) closely parallels an extensive body of research on nondual awareness (NDA).

Nondual Awareness: Consciousness-as-Such
Josipovic, Z. (2019). "Nondual awareness: consciousness-as-such as non-representational reflexivity." Progress in Brain Research, 244, 273--298. Elsevier.

Josipovic defines nondual awareness as a non-conceptual, non-propositional awareness that knows itself without relying on mental representations. He proposes an expanded map of consciousness that includes, beyond contents and arousal levels, an "indeterminate substrate" and consciousness-as-such. This consciousness-as-such is phenomenologically, cognitively, and neurobiologically a unique kind—distinct from all contents, functions, and states.

This maps to the framework's Ø · ⧉ ≈ (null-reference, presence, field, resonance). Both describe an awareness that is revealed when mediators are relaxed, not constructed through effort.

Nondual Awareness and Minimal Phenomenal Experience
Josipovic, Z. & Miskovic, V. (2020). "Nondual Awareness and Minimal Phenomenal Experience." Frontiers in Psychology, 11, 2087.

This paper distinguishes nondual awareness from "minimal phenomenal experiences" (states of reduced content). The authors argue that NDA is not simply the absence of content but a positive mode of awareness—present, self-knowing, and non-representational. This parallels the framework's insistence that null (Ø) is not emptiness but what remains when interference is withdrawn.

Without Birth, Without Death
Josipovic, Z. (2025). "Without birth, without death—issues in the research of nondual awareness or consciousness itself." Frontiers in Psychology, 16, 1665826.

Josipovic's most recent paper warns that researchers and participants are increasingly eager to label various states as nondual awareness, when the genuine article is rare and difficult to recognize without conceptual mediation. This maps to the framework's guardrail: claiming arrival at null is itself ⊘ᵢ. Both recognize that the reification of the state destroys the state.

1.4 Hedonic Adaptation and the Oscillation Cycle

The framework's observation that all post-survival action is sensation-seeking—and that the cycle of rest-unrest-rest drives all activity—is supported by decades of hedonic adaptation research.

The Hedonic Treadmill
Brickman, P. & Campbell, D.T. (1971). "Hedonic relativism and planning the good society." In Adaptation-level theory: A symposium, 287--305. Academic Press. Subsequently validated in: Diener, E. et al. (2006). "Beyond the hedonic treadmill: Revising the adaptation theory of well-being." American Psychologist, 61, 305--314.

The hedonic treadmill describes a homeostatic mechanism by which subjective well-being returns to baseline after positive or negative events. Sustained pleasure becomes neutral; sustained pain diminishes. The system continually seeks new stimulation. This is the framework's Ø → ⊘ → Ø cycle described in psychological terms: a permanent state becomes imperceptible, driving the organism to seek the next state.

Dynamical Models of Hedonic Cycling
Carrero, J.A. et al. (2024). "Running on the Hedonic Treadmill: A Dynamical Model of Happiness Based on an Approach--Avoidance Framework." Journal of Happiness Studies, 25, 766.

This paper models happiness as oscillation using dynamical systems mathematics, showing that phases of pleasure and displeasure repeatedly alternate as a structural feature of approach-avoidance motivation. The authors find that self-centred seeking of pleasure maximization induces fluctuating happiness—a formal confirmation of the framework's claim that the wave cannot be stopped from within the wave.

1.5 Anticipation Over Reward

The framework's insight that anticipation is the dominant sensation—not arrival—is robustly confirmed by dopamine research.

Sustained Dopamine During Anticipation
Fotros, A. et al. (2023). "Anticipation of Appetitive Operant Action Induces Sustained Dopamine Release in the Nucleus Accumbens." Journal of Neuroscience, 43(21), 3922--3932.

This study directly measured dopamine release during anticipation versus reward receipt. Rats showed sustained dopamine plateau throughout the entire cue period preceding reward—but not at reward delivery. The anticipation, not the outcome, produced the dominant neurochemical signal.

Reward Prediction and Gambling
Linnet, J. (2014). "Neurobiological underpinnings of reward anticipation and outcome evaluation in gambling disorder." Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience, 8, 100.

This review maps dopaminergic dysfunction in gambling to the distinction between reward prediction (anticipation) and reward prediction error (outcome evaluation). It confirms that the dopamine system is primarily an anticipation system—the pursuit, not the arrival, drives behaviour. This is the framework's observation that the gambler seeks the tension, not the resolution.

Framework

Anticipation is the primary product. Resolution resets the cycle. The pursuit is the sensation being sought.

Neuroscience

Dopamine release is greater during anticipation than reward receipt. Uncertainty amplifies the signal further.

Part II — Where Published Research Covers Ground the Framework Does Not

The framework is deliberately non-mechanistic. It describes structure and dynamics without mapping to biological substrates. Published research fills these gaps.

2.1 Neural Correlates and Measurement

Specific Neural Signatures
Seth, A.K. et al. (2006); Cebolla & Cheron (2019); Ruhnau, Hauswald & Weisz (2014, Frontiers in Psychology).

Consciousness research identifies specific oscillatory frequencies (gamma 12--70 Hz, alpha 7--13 Hz), brain regions (thalamocortical system, precuneus network), and connectivity patterns as correlates of conscious experience. The framework does not map its concepts to neural architecture—Ø, ≈, and ⧉ are described experientially, not neurobiologically. This is a deliberate choice (mechanism visibility without mechanism specification), but it means the framework cannot generate predictions testable by neuroimaging.

2.2 The Combination Problem

How Micro-Consciousness Combines
Hunt, T. (2025). Cycles Upon Cycles; Tononi, G. et al. (2016). Integrated Information Theory.

GRT and Integrated Information Theory address how separate oscillating systems combine into unified conscious experience. GRT proposes that shared resonance allows combination without extinction of constituent parts. The framework's field concept (⧉) implies non-local awareness but does not address how individual fields merge, separate, or interact at boundaries.

2.3 Quantitative Instruments

Measuring Nondual Awareness
Garland, E.L. et al. (2018). "The Nondual Awareness Dimensional Assessment (NADA)." Mindfulness, 9(6), 1765--1779.

The NADA measures nondual awareness along two dimensions: self-transcendence and bliss. It enables empirical comparison across populations and interventions. The framework explicitly rejects measurement as a guardrail (progress tracking = ⊘ᵢ). This is philosophically consistent—measuring the state may reintroduce the interference the framework describes. But it also means the framework cannot participate in the empirical validation cycle that peer-reviewed science requires.

2.4 Clinical and Corrective Debiasing

Structured Bias Mitigation
Fasolo, B., Heard, C. & Scopelliti, I. (2024). "Mitigating cognitive bias to improve organizational decisions." Journal of Management. Also: Croskerry, P. et al. (2013). "Cognitive debiasing." BMJ Quality & Safety.

Academic debiasing research identifies two approaches: training/awareness (debiasing) and environmental restructuring (choice architecture). These involve active intervention—curricula, warnings, feedback loops, structured decision processes. The framework's approach—recognition alone permits return (↺)—operates at a different level. Published research would classify this as an untested hypothesis about "awareness-based debiasing" that lacks experimental validation.

2.5 Phenomenological Methodology

The Problem of Self-Report
Krägeloh, C.U. (2019). "Phenomenological Research Fails to Capture the Experience of Nondual Awareness." Mindfulness, 10, 15--25. Also: Josipovic, Z. (2025), Frontiers in Psychology.

Academic research struggles with how to study nondual awareness when language inevitably distorts the experience. Josipovic warns that participants and researchers eagerly label various states as nondual awareness without genuine recognition. The framework acknowledges this through ⊘ₗ (linguistic interference) but does not offer a methodology for third-person verification. How would a researcher distinguish someone genuinely in resonant cognition from someone who has merely learned the vocabulary?

Part III — Where the Framework Covers Ground Published Research Does Not

These are areas where the framework makes claims or provides perspectives not found in the academic literature reviewed.

3.1 The "Why" Before the "What"

Original Contribution

Academic work describes oscillation, measures it, and correlates it. GRT maps cycles from quantum to cosmic scale. Neural oscillation research identifies frequency bands. But almost none asks why the wave moves at all.

The framework begins there: contrast is the precondition for experience, not a discovery within experience. Oscillation is not explained by a prior cause—it is what "existing" means. No published paper in the reviewed literature makes this foundational move as its starting point.

3.2 Fulfillment as Sensation, Not a Separate Category

Original Contribution

Psychology maintains a categorical distinction between hedonic wellbeing (pleasure-based) and eudaimonic wellbeing (meaning-based). Research programmes and measurement instruments are built around this division.

The framework dissolves this: fulfillment registers as felt experience, activates the same reward circuitry, and fades if sustained without interruption. It is sensation at a different frequency, not a different kind. No published paper in the reviewed literature makes this collapse explicitly. The Self-Centred/Selflessness Happiness Model (Dambrun & Ricard, 2011) approaches it by distinguishing fluctuating from stable happiness, but retains the hedonic/eudaimonic distinction.

3.3 The Large Curve Insight

Original Contribution

The framework proposes that what appears as stillness may be oscillation at a wavelength too vast for human perception—a curve so large it appears as a straight line from within a human lifespan. This implies that null (Ø) is not absence of pattern but pattern at a resolution beyond ordinary detection.

GRT discusses nested hierarchies across scale but never proposes this specific inversion: that the "ground state" of awareness may itself be oscillation at cosmological wavelength. This is a genuinely novel contribution with no direct parallel in the reviewed literature.

3.4 Sacrifice as Preferred Frequency

Original Contribution

Published psychology treats sacrifice, discipline, and austerity through self-regulation and willpower models—effortful override of preference. The framework proposes that what appears as sacrifice from one perspective is the practitioner's preferred oscillation. The monk does not overcome the desire for comfort; the absence of discipline is more uncomfortable than discipline itself. Mastery shifts the baseline.

This removes moral hierarchy from the oscillation without removing discernment. No published framework reviewed makes this specific move.

3.5 Bias Removal Through Mechanism Visibility

Original Contribution

All published debiasing research is corrective: train people, warn them, restructure their choices. The framework proposes that seeing the mechanism of bias—recognizing that one is oscillating at a particular frequency shaped by inheritance and grip—is itself the debiasing. Recognition alone permits ↺. Nothing is corrected; the pattern becomes transparent.

Academic approach:  bias detected → intervention → correction
Framework approach: bias mechanism seen → grip loosens → ↺ (natural return)

This approach has not been experimentally tested but represents a fundamentally different theory of how bias dissolves.

3.6 Self-Falsifying Guardrails

Original Contribution

The framework builds in structural conditions under which the framework itself becomes interference: attempting to teach it produces ⊘ᵢ + ⊘ₗ, attempting to hold it produces ⊘, claiming permanent arrival produces ⊘ᵢ. No published consciousness framework reviewed includes such self-falsification conditions. Most frameworks attempt to be robust; this one attempts to be transparent about its own potential for misuse.

3.7 The Unified Oscillation Symbol

Original Contribution

Josipovic treats nondual awareness as "a unique kind" distinct from phenomenal contents. GRT treats resonance as a mechanism that produces consciousness. Both maintain a structural distinction between awareness and what awareness observes.

The framework goes further: even the distinction between null (Ø) and resonance (≈) collapses at sufficient scale. They are phases of the same wave. The observer and the observed are the same oscillation, differentiated only by the grip that was claiming otherwise.

Part IV — Applications for Researchers

The following examples show how researchers in specific disciplines can use the framework to generate new hypotheses, design experiments, and extend existing work. Each example identifies the research gap, the framework concept that addresses it, and a concrete study design.

4.1 Neuroscience of Consciousness

Research gap

GRT maps oscillatory hierarchies but does not explain why oscillation occurs at all. Neural correlate studies identify which frequencies accompany consciousness but not why consciousness requires variation.

Framework concept

Contrast as the condition for experience: a permanent state becomes imperceptible. The nervous system requires change to register anything.

Study design

Hypothesis: Sustained identical neural stimulation at any frequency will produce diminishing conscious experience over time, regardless of frequency band, because consciousness requires contrast rather than any particular oscillatory signature.

Method: Use transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS) at fixed frequencies (alpha, beta, gamma) while measuring subjective reports and neural markers of awareness. Vary the duration from seconds to minutes. Predict: sustained identical stimulation at even the "optimal" consciousness frequency will show adaptation—supporting the framework's claim that contrast, not frequency, is fundamental.

Contribution: This would test whether consciousness requires change in oscillation rather than oscillation at a particular frequency—a distinction the current literature does not make.

4.2 Psychology and Wellbeing

Research gap

Hedonic and eudaimonic wellbeing are measured and studied as distinct constructs. Research programmes, interventions, and measurement instruments maintain this division. But does it hold at the neurochemical level?

Framework concept

Fulfillment is sensation at a different frequency, not a different category. All action post-survival is sensation-seeking at varying time scales.

Study design

Hypothesis: Eudaimonic experiences (meaning, purpose, mastery) show the same hedonic adaptation curves as hedonic experiences (pleasure, comfort, novelty), differing only in cycle length—not in kind.

Method: Longitudinal experience sampling of individuals pursuing meaning-based goals (volunteering, artistic mastery, spiritual practice) alongside pleasure-based goals. Measure subjective wellbeing at intervals from days to months. Predict: both show adaptation to baseline, but eudaimonic activities adapt on longer cycles. The structure is identical; the frequency differs.

Contribution: If confirmed, this would challenge the hedonic/eudaimonic dichotomy foundational to positive psychology and suggest a unified oscillation model of wellbeing.

4.3 Contemplative Science

Research gap

Josipovic (2025) warns that self-reports of nondual awareness are unreliable because participants learn the vocabulary and label ordinary states as NDA. How can genuine nondual awareness be distinguished from conceptual imitation?

Framework concept

The framework's collapse conditions: if someone is tracking progress, they are in ⊘ᵢ. If they are explaining the state, they are in ⊘ₗ. Genuine resonant cognition has no observer reporting on it—because the observer-observed split is itself interference.

Study design

Hypothesis: Genuine nondual awareness will correlate with reduced self-referential processing (default mode network deactivation), while conceptual imitation of NDA will show increased self-referential processing (monitoring whether one is "in the state").

Method: Compare long-term meditators and newcomers trained in NDA vocabulary. Use fMRI to measure default mode network activity during reported NDA states. Predict: newcomers show paradoxical DMN activation during "NDA" reports (they are monitoring). Long-term practitioners show DMN deactivation (genuine observer-observed dissolution). This operationalises the framework's distinction between ≈ and ⊘ᵢ.

Contribution: Provides a neurobiological criterion for distinguishing genuine from imitative nondual awareness—addressing Josipovic's key concern about participant report validity.

4.4 Cognitive Bias and Decision-Making

Research gap

All published debiasing interventions are corrective (training, warnings, choice architecture). No study has tested whether awareness of the mechanism of bias—without corrective instruction—reduces biased decision-making.

Framework concept

Bias removal through mechanism visibility. The framework claims that seeing the structural origin of a bias (inherited frequency + identity attachment + grip) dissolves the bias without correction.

Study design

Hypothesis: Teaching participants the structural mechanism of bias (how preferences become identity, how identity becomes perceptual filter) reduces bias as effectively as teaching specific debiasing strategies—and with greater transfer to novel situations.

Method: Three groups. Group A receives standard debiasing training (strategy-based). Group B receives mechanism-awareness training (the framework's approach: understanding how frequency fixation + identity attachment + inherited patterning produces bias, without corrective strategies). Group C receives no training (control). All groups are tested on a battery of cognitive bias tasks including novel bias types not covered in training.

Prediction: Group A outperforms on trained bias types. Group B shows broader transfer to novel biases. The mechanism is different: Group A learns to correct specific errors; Group B learns to see the process of error formation, which generalises.

Contribution: If confirmed, this introduces a new category of debiasing intervention—"mechanism transparency"—distinct from both debiasing and choice architecture.

4.5 Philosophy of Mind

Research gap

The hard problem of consciousness asks why physical processes give rise to subjective experience. Current approaches (physicalism, panpsychism, dualism) all assume a distinction between the physical and the experiential that must then be bridged.

Framework concept

The framework proposes that oscillation is experience—not that it produces or correlates with experience. At sufficient scale, null (Ø) and resonance (≈) converge. The observer and observed are the same oscillation. There is no bridge needed because there are not two sides.

Philosophical application

The framework offers a position distinct from standard panpsychism (which attributes experience to all matter) and from emergentism (which derives experience from complexity). It proposes that experience is not a property of oscillating systems but is what oscillation is when not claimed by an observer. The grip (⊘ᵢ) creates the apparent division between subject and object. Without the grip, there is oscillation—which is neither physical nor mental but prior to both categories.

Philosophers working on the combination problem, the hard problem, or the relationship between panpsychism and non-dualism could explore whether the framework's "grip" concept offers a parsimonious alternative to current bridge theories.

4.6 Education and Pedagogy

Research gap

Educational psychology studies motivation, engagement, and attention through cognitive and behavioural models. The role of inherited perceptual biases in learning—and the possibility of teaching students to see the mechanism of their own cognitive filters—is underexplored.

Framework concept

The framework's identity inquiry (Layer 1 functional identity vs. Layer 2 divisive identity) and its children's story application ("The Strings at School") demonstrate how cognitive-emotional patterns can be made visible to young learners without psychological jargon.

Study design

Hypothesis: Teaching children to recognise "strings" (automatic emotional-cognitive pulls) as patterns rather than commands improves social-emotional outcomes and reduces reactive behaviour.

Method: Implement a classroom programme based on the framework's stories and interference-recognition approach. Compare with standard social-emotional learning (SEL) curriculum. Measure: reactive behaviour incidents, self-reported emotional regulation, peer relationship quality, ability to distinguish banter from harm.

Contribution: Tests whether mechanism-visibility (seeing the "strings") is more effective than technique-based emotional regulation (deep breathing, counting to ten) for age-appropriate populations.

4.7 Social Neuroscience and Empathy

Research gap

Inter-brain synchronisation research (Valencia & Froese, 2020) has demonstrated neural coupling during social interaction but has not investigated the role of identity grip in disrupting synchronisation. Current models focus on sensory and motor coupling, not on ownership and control dynamics.

Framework concept

The framework predicts that co-resonance (≈ᵢ ∥ ≈ⱼ) degrades when either party shifts from resonance to ownership (⊘ᵢ)—attempting to control, fix, or possess the other's frequency. Love becomes interference when one system tries to stabilise the other.

Study design

Hypothesis: Inter-brain neural synchronisation decreases when one participant shifts from empathic listening to advice-giving, problem-solving, or emotional control of the other.

Method: Hyperscanning (dual EEG) during dyadic conversations. Phase 1: open empathic listening (predicted high synchrony). Phase 2: one participant instructed to "help" by giving advice, correcting, or fixing (predicted synchrony drop). Phase 3: return to open listening (predicted synchrony recovery). Code verbal behaviour for control/ownership markers.

Prediction: The shift from parallel presence (≈ᵢ ∥ ≈ⱼ) to identity-based intervention (⊘ᵢ) will show measurable desynchronisation—operationalising the framework's claim that ownership disrupts co-resonance.

Contribution: Extends inter-brain synchronisation research from sensory/motor coupling to identity and ownership dynamics, opening a new dimension of social neuroscience investigation.
