# Chapter 4: The Quiet Between

---

Saturday was the first day in a long time that Kavi woke up without the hum.

He noticed it because of its absence — like how you only hear the refrigerator when it stops. He lay in bed and checked: Chest? Quiet. Stomach? Still. Hands? Just hands. Head? Clear, like a whiteboard before anyone writes on it.

It lasted about nine seconds.

Then his brain said *you don't have the hum!* and the chest string hummed lightly, like it heard its name and wanted to respond. Then the head string chimed in: *wait, is this normal? Is something wrong? Why don't I feel the usual thing?*

And just like that, the quiet was gone. Not because anything happened. But because he'd noticed the quiet, and noticing it was already a thing happening.

Kavi groaned into his pillow.

---

He told Thatha about it over morning tea on the porch.

"I had it," Kavi said. "The quiet. For like nine seconds. And then I noticed I had it, and that ruined it."

"Hmm," Thatha said.

"It's like... you can't look at it directly. The second you try to look at the quiet, it stops being quiet because you're looking."

Thatha held his tea with both hands. Steam curled upward. "Have you ever tried to see a star that's very faint?"

"What?"

"At night. The very faint stars. The ones at the edge of what you can see."

"I guess."

"If you look directly at them, they disappear. But if you look slightly to the side — not at the star, but near it — you can see it."

Kavi squinted. "That's real?"

"That's real. Your eye has different parts. The center is good for bright things. The edges are better for faint things. If you want to see something very faint, you have to not look at it directly."

"That's weird."

"It's how eyes work. Maybe it's how other things work too."

---

Kavi spent the rest of Saturday trying not to look at the quiet directly.

This was harder than it sounded, because "trying not to look" is still a kind of looking. It's like someone saying "don't think about elephants" — the elephant is already there, stomping around your brain with its big gray body.

He tried during breakfast. Meera was talking about a group chat drama, and Appa was reading the news on his phone, and Amma was eating toast standing up because she was Amma and sitting was optional. Kavi sat in the middle of all of this and tried to find the quiet between things.

It wasn't there. Everything was noise and movement and cereal.

He tried in the backyard. Thatha was weeding the garden, moving slowly from plant to plant like each one was a patient. Kavi sat on the steps and tried to be still.

Strings: Chest at 2. Head at 3 — a low murmur of scattered thoughts (*I should text Dex, what's for lunch, I wonder if Ms. Leona is grading the posters, what if she saw the smiley face on Jupiter*). Stomach at 1. Hands wanting to fidget.

Not quiet. Just quieter.

He sat with it. The sun was warm on his arms. A bee moved between the flowers Thatha was weeding around, doing its own thing without caring about anything else. The hose dripped from where Appa hadn't turned it off all the way.

Drip. Drip. Drip.

Between each drip, there was a gap. A tiny pause where there was no drip. Kavi focused on the gap — not on the drip, but on the space between drips.

Something shifted.

It wasn't dramatic. Nothing opened up, no white light, no cosmic understanding. But for a moment — not nine seconds this time, maybe three — the strings weren't pulling and he wasn't watching them and the space between the drips became the space between everything.

Between heartbeats. Between breaths. Between one thought and the next.

There was a gap there. And in the gap, he wasn't Kavi-who-was-too-serious or Kavi-who-yelled-at-Rohan or Kavi-who-had-weird-feelings-in-his-chest. He was just... here. Like the bee. Like the drip between drips.

Then Thatha's knee popped as he stood up, and Kavi laughed, and the moment dissolved like sugar in tea.

But he'd felt it. Not for nine seconds this time — for three. But different from the first time, because this time he hadn't noticed it by accident. He'd found it. Not by looking directly. By looking at the space next to it.

---

"Thatha, I found it again."

Thatha was washing his hands at the outside tap. "The quiet?"

"Yeah. But only for a second. And I wasn't looking at it."

Thatha dried his hands on his gardening pants, which made Amma crazy but was a permanent feature of Thatha's personality.

"The dripping," Kavi said. "I was listening to the dripping, and instead of listening to the drips, I listened to the spaces between them. And the quiet was there."

Thatha looked at him for a long moment. Not evaluating. More like... recognizing. Like he'd been waiting for Kavi to arrive at a place he already knew existed.

"What did it feel like?"

"Like I didn't need anything. Not in a sad way. In a... full way? Like I was already wherever I needed to be."

"And the strings?"

"I don't think they were gone. I think they were just... not loud. Like they were asleep."

"Or maybe," Thatha said, "they didn't need to pull. Because you were already here."

Kavi remembered what he'd said last week — about art class, how the strings went quiet because he was already where he wanted to be. "It's the same thing," he said. "As art."

"Is it?"

Kavi thought about it. "No. In art, I was doing something. My hands were busy, my brain was busy with colors and shapes. The strings were quiet because I was distracted from them."

"And the dripping?"

"I wasn't doing anything. I was just sitting. The strings were quiet because..." He paused. "Because I stopped pulling back."

"What do you mean?"

Kavi struggled with this. He could feel the idea — it was right there, like a word on the tip of his tongue — but the words kept slipping.

"The strings pull me. But I also pull the strings. Like, the chest string pulls me toward worrying, but I'm also holding onto the worry. If I let go — not push it away, just... let go — the string goes slack."

Thatha sat down on the porch step next to Kavi. For a long time, neither of them said anything.

A cardinal landed on the fence. Stayed for two seconds. Flew away.

"You know what's funny?" Kavi said. "The quiet isn't something I have to create. It's already there. Between the drips. Between the heartbeats. I just have to stop filling it."

Thatha didn't say anything. He didn't need to. The smile was enough — not the "how cute" kind or the "good question" kind, but something quieter and older than either of those.

---

That night, Kavi couldn't sleep.

Not because of the strings — they were at a manageable 2 across the board, nothing dramatic. He couldn't sleep because his brain was doing the thing it did when it had a new toy: running loops, testing theories, poking the idea from every angle.

*The quiet is between things. It's always there. But the strings fill it up. And I fill it up by holding the strings.*

*But if I let go...*

*But you can't try to let go. Trying is another string.*

*So how do you let go without trying?*

He turned over. Then turned back. Meera's music was faintly audible through the wall — something with acoustic guitar and a girl's voice. Downstairs, Appa was watching a cricket match, the distant crowd noise rising and falling like breathing.

Kavi did something he'd never done before. He stopped trying to figure it out.

Not because he decided to stop. More like his brain got tired of running the loop and just... gave up. The way you stop trying to remember a word, and then ten minutes later the word just appears.

And in the giving up, in the stopping, in the moment where his brain went from "working on the problem" to "not working on anything" — there it was.

The quiet.

Not between drips this time. Not between breaths. Just... the quiet that was always underneath everything, like the silence under music, like the white page under the ink.

His body settled. The strings were there — he could feel them distantly, like hearing traffic from inside a warm house — but they weren't asking for anything. They were just strings. Not pulling. Not needing. Just there, the way his arms were just there and his legs were just there.

Kavi fell asleep inside the quiet, and for the first time in as long as he could remember, he didn't dream about anything at all.

---

He woke on Sunday with a drawing in his head.

Before breakfast, before brushing his teeth, he grabbed his sketchbook and drew. The gingerbread boy again — but this time, the strings were loose. Not cut. Not gone. Slack. And in the space between the strings, he drew something he didn't have a word for yet: just open space. Empty, but not the scary kind of empty. The kind of empty that a room has when it's clean and the windows are open and the light comes in.

He wrote under it: *The quiet isn't the opposite of the strings. It's what's there when the strings aren't pulling.*

Then, underneath that, smaller: *And sometimes it's there even when they are.*

## Still Thinking?

- Kavi found the quiet between the drips. Is there a sound or a rhythm you know well enough to find the gaps in it? The space between footsteps, between car sounds, between someone's words?

- "You can't look at the quiet directly." Have you ever noticed that trying hard to relax makes it harder? Why do you think that is?

- Kavi says the quiet "isn't something I have to create — it's already there. I just have to stop filling it." What are the things you fill your quiet with? Are they on purpose or automatic?

- At the end, Kavi fell asleep in the quiet. Have you ever had a moment — right before sleep, or in a car, or somewhere safe — where everything went still and it felt okay? What was that like?
