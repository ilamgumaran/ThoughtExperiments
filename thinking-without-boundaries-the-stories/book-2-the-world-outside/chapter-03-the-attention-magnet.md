# Chapter 3: The Attention Magnet

---

Nila started keeping a notebook.

Not for school — for Marcus. Or rather, for the pattern she was starting to see. She wrote "OBSERVATIONS" on the front in black Sharpie and kept it in the inside pocket of her jacket where nobody would see it.

She was going to be a scientist about this. No fire. No reaction. Just data.

---

**Day 1 — Monday**

**8:22 AM:** Marcus arrives late. Makes a joke about Mrs. Callahan's handwriting. ("Is that a Q or a tornado?") Six kids laugh. Mrs. Callahan ignores it. Marcus's face: brief disappointment. He tries again: "Seriously, I think there's a weather warning in paragraph two." Eight kids laugh. Mrs. Callahan: "That's enough." Marcus's face: satisfaction.

**10:15 AM:** Recess. Marcus takes Aiden's basketball and holds it above his head. Aiden is short. Aiden jumps for it. Marcus laughs. Three other boys laugh. One boy says "give it back, man." Marcus gives it back after twelve seconds. Nila notes: the twelve seconds weren't about the basketball. They were about watching Aiden jump.

**12:00 PM:** Lunch. Marcus goes to Lily's table and sits in her seat while she's in the bathroom. When Lily comes back, he says "this is MY seat now" and won't move. Lily stands there, tray in hand, face reddening. Two kids at the table are uncomfortable. One is grinning. Nila's fire lights — a 6 — but she writes instead of yelling. She writes: *Marcus's eyes are on Lily's face. He's watching her blush. That's the thing he wants.*

Mrs. Callahan walks by. Marcus moves. Lily sits. The moment evaporates.

**2:30 PM:** Art class. Marcus is quiet. He's drawing something — Nila can't see what. His face is different. Focused. Not scanning the room. Not performing. He looks like a completely different person.

Nila writes: *Art class. Marcus didn't do anything. Not one thing. Why?*

---

**Day 2 — Tuesday**

**8:30 AM:** Marcus gives Jaden a nickname: "Robot." Because Jaden is quiet and precise and doesn't react much. Marcus says it four times in twenty minutes. "Hey Robot, what's the answer?" "Robot knows." "Somebody charge Robot, he's running low." Jaden says nothing. His face shows nothing. His neck doesn't even go red.

Marcus stops after the fourth time. Nila writes: *Jaden gave him nothing. Marcus stopped.*

**11:00 AM:** Marcus tries the nickname on Priya. "Hey, Priya Perfect." Priya looks at him. Doesn't smile, doesn't frown. Says "Good morning, Marcus" in the same tone she'd use with the principal. Marcus blinks. Tries again: "I mean, you're like ANNOYINGLY perfect." Priya says, "Thank you" and turns back to her book.

Marcus walks away. Nila writes: *Priya gave him nothing too. But different from Jaden. Jaden shut down. Priya just... didn't play. Same result: Marcus left.*

**1:00 PM:** Marcus trips Nila in the hallway. Not hard — a foot-out-and-pull-back thing that makes her stumble but not fall. Nila's fire goes to a 9 instantly. Her face burns. She spins around and Marcus is already grinning, already watching, already collecting.

"WHAT IS YOUR PROBLEM?"

Marcus's grin widens. "Whoa, chill."

"Don't TELL me to chill. You just tripped me."

"It was an accident."

"It was NOT an accident."

Kids are watching. The hallway has stopped. The spotlight is on, and Nila is center stage, and Marcus is in the wings, grinning, feeding.

She knows this. She KNOWS this. She wrote it in her notebook yesterday: *he wants the reaction.* But knowing it and stopping it are two completely different things when the fire is at a 9 and the justice alarm is screaming.

Mrs. Callahan appears from somewhere. "Both of you. Keep walking."

Nila keeps walking. Her legs are shaking. Marcus keeps walking in the other direction, whistling.

She writes in her notebook with a hand that's still trembling: *When it's about ME, I can't watch. The fire takes over before the scientist can get to the glass.*

---

**Day 3 — Wednesday**

Nila sits at the kitchen table before school, staring at her notebook. Two days of data. A pattern is forming, and she doesn't like what it shows.

She makes a chart:

| Target | Reaction | Marcus Response | Duration |
|--------|----------|----------------|----------|
| Jaden | None (shutdown) | Lost interest | Short |
| Priya | None (calm redirect) | Walked away | Very short |
| Lily | Embarrassment + freeze | Watched closely, grinned | Long |
| Aiden | Jumped, frustrated | Laughed, held basketball longer | Medium |
| Nila | Full explosion | Widest grin, escalated | Longest |

The chart is devastating. It says what Nila doesn't want to hear:

The bigger the reaction, the more Marcus likes it. The longer the show runs.

And Nila gives the biggest reaction of anyone.

She is, she realizes with a sinking feeling in her stomach, Marcus's favorite channel. The one with the most drama, the most intensity, the most entertainment value. Her fire — the thing she's most proud of, the thing that makes her HER — is exactly what makes her the best target.

"That's not fair," she says out loud to the empty kitchen.

The kitchen doesn't answer. Paati comes in and starts the coffee.

"Paati, it's not fair."

"What's not fair?"

"The reason Marcus messes with me the most is because I react the most. So the thing that makes me care is the thing that makes me a target. It's like being punished for having feelings."

Paati poured water through the filter. Slow. Precise. The coffee dripped.

"You are not punished for feelings," Paati said. "Feelings are..." She searched. "Good. But feelings with no —" She made a steering wheel motion. "Feelings with no driving — they go everywhere. Hit everything."

"So I need to control my feelings?"

"No." Paati was firm on this. "Not control. Controlling feelings is..." She pressed her hands together hard, showing compression. "Push down. Makes worse."

"Then what?"

Paati held up the coffee filter. The water was dripping through it — slowly, naturally, gravity doing the work.

"Let through. Not hold. Not push. Let through."

"But if I let the fire through, I yell at Marcus."

"That is not letting through. That is" — she mimed an explosion, hands bursting outward — "that is throwing at him. Letting through is" — she pointed at the coffee dripping — "the fire comes in. You feel it. It goes through you. Out the bottom. Like coffee. What is left is" — she tapped the cup — "this. Clear. Strong. Not burning."

Nila looked at the coffee dripping into the cup. Each drop was dark, rich, full of everything the grounds had given it. But it wasn't grounds anymore. It was filtered. The flavor stayed. The grit was gone.

"You're saying I can feel the fire without throwing it?"

"You feel everything. Big antenna." Paati tapped her head. "But antenna receives. Antenna doesn't throw back. You receive the fire. You feel it. You let it go through. What stays is the caring without the burning."

---

Nila went to school with the coffee filter in her head.

**8:30 AM:** Marcus called her "Nila the Nag" in homeroom. Her chest lit — immediate, volcanic, a 7 at least. She felt the fire rise, felt it hit her throat, felt the words forming—

She breathed.

Not the deep-breath thing that every adult tells you to do, which usually just means you yell slightly later. A different breath. She let the fire in — really let it in, felt the full heat of it, the injustice, the wrongness — and then she imagined it dripping through her, the way coffee drips through the filter.

*The fire is here. I feel it. It's going through.*

The fire didn't vanish. It dimmed from a 7 to a 4. The words in her throat dissolved. The urge to yell didn't disappear — it softened into something else. Not silence. A choice.

She looked at Marcus. He was waiting. His face had the anticipatory gleam of someone watching a firework's fuse burn down.

Nila didn't yell. She didn't say "that's not funny." She didn't give him the show.

She turned back to her notebook and wrote: *He called me a name. The fire was a 7. I let it through. It went to a 4. He's still waiting.*

She could feel his gaze. For about ten seconds, Marcus watched her not react. Then she felt the exact moment he looked away — a shift in the room's energy, a signal her antenna caught. He looked away, and the firework fizzled, and the show didn't happen.

**Nila wrote: *It worked. Not perfectly — the fire is still here, and it's still a 4, and part of me is furious that I didn't say anything. But he stopped. He literally stopped because there was nothing to watch.*

**He's not a magnet. I am. My reaction is the magnet. And I just turned it off. Not all the way. But a little. And a little was enough.**

## Still Thinking?

- Nila's chart shows that the bigger the reaction, the longer Marcus persists. Think about someone who teases or annoys. Do they do it more with certain people? What's different about those people?

- Jaden gave nothing by shutting down. Priya gave nothing by staying calm and kind. Same result, different methods. Which feels more natural to you? Is there a difference between shutting down and choosing not to engage?

- Nila says being punished for having feelings "isn't fair." Is it fair? Is there a way to keep the feelings without giving someone the show?

- Paati's coffee filter: the fire goes in, you feel it, it drips through, and what's left is "the caring without the burning." Does that make sense to you? Have you ever felt something strong and let it pass through without throwing it back?

- Nila turned off the magnet "not all the way — but a little. And a little was enough." Have you ever tried not reacting to something that usually gets a big reaction from you? What happened?
