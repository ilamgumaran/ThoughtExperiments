# Resonant Cognition Framework -- Thinking Without Boundaries

**Authors:** Ilamgumaran Velayuthan Karunanithi & Claude AI

This repository carries the **Resonant Cognition Framework** -- a psychology-of-cognition theory of mind built around how attention, identity, desire, and "interference" shape human experience. It is the foundational cognition source from which the rest of the family flows: from understanding how minds actually work, to orchestrating them in collaboration with AI, to running engineering organizations on those principles, to operating those organizations day-to-day with agents.

## Canonical concepts owned here

The framework introduces a small vocabulary that is its own. These terms are *not* HIO terms; HIO and this framework are kindred but independent:

- **Resonance** -- the natural state of attention before interference
- **Contraction (↓)** -- withdrawal of fueling, distinct from suppression or dissociation
- **Null (Ø)** -- unmediated occurring; presence without an owner
- **Strings, bells, the puppy, the spotlight** -- canonical metaphors used across the stories
- **Identity oscillation** -- how psychological identity arises and dissolves
- **Sign operator** -- desire framed as toward (resonance) or away (suffering)

## Why this repo informs HIO

The HIO Framework starts from the premise that any organized human endeavor is now a **cognitive ecosystem** -- humans (organic intelligence) and AI (inorganic intelligence) working together. To orchestrate cognitive ecosystems well, you need a clear theory of cognition itself. This repo supplies that theory in a form built specifically around how attention works: what fuels noise, what dissolves it, what counts as the unmediated present.

The vocabulary is independent on purpose. HIO talks about *organic* / *inorganic intelligence*, *harmonization*, *emergence*. This repo talks about *resonance*, *contraction*, *null*. They are kindred but not synonymous; each is canonical in its own context. See [`AGENTS.md`](AGENTS.md) for how to translate (and how *not* to translate) between the two when working across repos.
